What the data shows

Figures over the full history, whatever range is selected

  1. House prices are up 144 times since 1953, from £1,891 to £272,751. That is 7.1% a year before inflation. Prices peaked in August 2022, just before mortgage rates jumped.
  2. The sharpest fall was the financial crisis. Prices were down 16.5% on a year earlier in early 2009. The fastest rise was 42% in the 1972 boom.
  3. Borrowing costs fell for 30 years, then reversed. The 10-year gilt yield went from 12.2% in 1990 to 0.25% in 2020, and is back at 4.5%.
  4. The pound has lost almost half its value against the dollar since 1971, from $2.41 to $1.34. It fell 10% in the two months after the 2016 EU referendum.
  5. A weaker pound makes imports dearer. Since January 2016, gold has risen 3.9× in dollars but 4.2× in pounds.
Sources: house prices, Nationwide (datasets/house-prices-uk); 10-year gilt yield, OECD (datasets/bond-yields-uk-10y); exchange rate, Federal Reserve (datasets/exchange-rates); gold (datasets/gold-prices); Brent crude, EIA (datasets/oil-prices).
Prices in pounds are converted from dollars at each month's average exchange rate. House prices are quarterly and in nominal terms. Built by Abhishek Pandya with Python and hand-drawn SVG; the same data model is provided for Power BI.
